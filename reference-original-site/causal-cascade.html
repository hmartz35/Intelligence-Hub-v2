Why this is dangerous

The numbers on the left look authoritative. They are not. Here is what is wrong with using a simulation like this to trade real money:

1
The model has no ground truth. The CPT weights were chosen by an analyst to illustrate causal reasoning. They were never fit to historical conflict data because no such dataset exists at this scope.
2
"Edge" is fabricated. The "model probability" comes from a static causal graph; the "market probability" comes from real traders with information the graph doesn't encode. The gap is mostly noise plus the traders knowing things you don't.
3
Selection bias compounds. The model "agrees" with the market on most contracts. The ones where it disagrees most loudly are exactly the ones where it's most likely to be wrong — markets price in information the model wasn't designed to see.
4
You are not the smart money. The traders on the other side of these contracts include hedge funds, specialist firms, and people with regional language access. A lone undergraduate with a Bayesian network is not bringing new information.
5
Visual confidence is not statistical confidence. The neon green numbers look like sports betting picks for a reason — that aesthetic borrows the language of urgency to pressure decisions. Sober tools rarely look like this.
